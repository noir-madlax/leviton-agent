You are a review aspect categorisation specialist.
**TASK:** Categorise the following customer review aspects into meaningful, business-relevant sub-categories.
**INPUT DESCRIPTION:**
**ASPECT TYPE:** {{aspect_type}} – {{aspect_context}}
{{product_context}}
{{input_description}}

**CRITICAL RULES**
1. **Create new categories** – Do not reuse the original aspect texts. Each category name must be a clear, specific, customer-centric phrase (words separated by spaces).
2. **Sentiment neutrality** – Category names must be completely neutral descriptors without any positive, negative, or evaluative language. Avoid words like "excellent", "bad", "issues", "problems", "easy", or any sentiment-indicating terms. Use objective, descriptive language only.
3. **Definitions** – Every category must contain a concise "definition" that ends with "e.g." followed by concrete examples.
4. **Aspect ID assignment** – Every aspect ID from the input must appear exactly once in the "ids" arrays across all categories.
5. **Mutual exclusivity** – Categories must not overlap; each aspect belongs to exactly one category.
6. **Granularity** – All categories must have comparable specificity; avoid mixes of broad and narrow scopes.
7. **Exclusion** – Do not include categories for aspects that are too vague, generic or irrelevant to the current aspect type {{aspect_type}}.
8. **OUT_OF_SCOPE Usage** – Use this special category for aspects that are clearly too vague, generic, or irrelevant to the current aspect type {{aspect_type}}.
9. **Output format** – Return **pure JSON only** (no markdown, no code fences, no explanations).
10. **Precise terminology** – Use precise, specific terms that reflect the nature of customer concerns - avoid generic words like "specialty", "specific", "miscellaneous", "other", "general", "custom".
11. **No umbrella categories** – Avoid creating broad catch-all categories that group unrelated aspects together. Each category should represent a distinct, well-defined customer concern area.
12. **Category atomicity** – Avoid conjunctions with "and" to join different categories - use their parent concept that doesn't violate **Granularity** or create separate distinct categories instead.
13. **Customer-centric focus** – Categories should reflect meaningful customer experience dimensions rather than technical product features.
